## 1. Experiment Overview

Experiment R5 addresses the Stage 1 extension question: **does injecting VLLM-generated visual captions into the retrieval query improve retrieval quality over the BGE-large text-only baseline?**

R1 through R4 completed the core Stage 1 ablation, arriving at the following optimised configuration:

- **Query strategy:** R1-C (multi-axis sub-queries)
- **Retrieval method:** Dense-only (ChromaDB cosine similarity)
- **k:** 6
- **Embedding model:** BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5
- **Context recall (R4-B):** 0.8945

R5 explores whether the VerdaSense pipeline can exploit the visual modality — photographs of wounds submitted alongside structured T.I.M.E. assessment inputs — to enrich the retrieval query with clinically grounded visual descriptions. Eight representative wound images (WT01–WT08) were pre-captioned by the VLLM, with each caption assigned to its corresponding wound type index in the testset. The captions provide structured four-axis visual observations (TISSUE, INFECTION, MOISTURE, EDGE) that partially mirror the T.I.M.E. assessment framework.

Four caption injection strategies were evaluated, differing only in *where* the caption is inserted within the R1-C multi-axis sub-query structure:

| Version | Caption Strategy | Sub-query Axes | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R5-A** | None (baseline) | A:2 B:3 C:2 (notes only) | Pure R1-C multi-axis retrieval. Cross-check vs R4-B. |
| **R5-B** | subC — context enrichment | A:2 B:3 C:2 (caption+notes) | VLLM caption replaces/augments Sub-query C. Notes+caption combined where notes exist; caption alone otherwise. |
| **R5-C** | subD — new 4th axis | A:2 B:3 C:2 (notes) D:2 (caption new axis) | Original R1-C preserved intact. New Sub-query D adds caption as an independent retrieval axis. Pool A+B+C+D = 9 results → capped at k=6. |
| **R5-D** | subBC — mechanism+context | A:2 B:3 (mech+caption) C:2 (notes+caption) | VLLM caption enriches both Sub-query B (dressing mechanism) and Sub-query C (context/notes). Sub-query A unchanged. |

R5-A simultaneously serves as a cross-check against R4-B, confirming pipeline continuity before interpreting the caption injection results. R5-B, R5-C, and R5-D represent three distinct hypotheses for how visual context could augment textual sub-queries — replacing a sub-query axis, adding a new one, or enriching multiple axes simultaneously.

**The central research question of R5: does visual grounding of the retrieval query, via structured VLLM captions, improve retrieval of relevant wound care guideline chunks?**

Cases with notes that contradict or extend the visual impression (e.g., a wound typed as WT03 but presenting with infection) receive a misaligned caption. This mismatch is the most likely mechanistic explanation for the retrieval degradation observed in R5-B and R5-D (discussed in Section 5).

The core question of R5 is whether each caption strategy adds net value. The table below reports all deltas relative to R5-A (no caption baseline), with a threshold of |Δ| > 0.005 to identify meaningful changes.

| NDCG@6 | 0.7271 | −0.0655 | 0.0000 | +0.0176 |

**Net contribution score (|Δ| > 0.005 threshold):**
- R5-B: **−6** (6 metrics hurt, 0 improved beyond threshold) → HURTS retrieval
- R5-C: **−2** (2 metrics hurt beyond threshold, 0 improved) → marginal net hurt
- R5-D: **−4** (4 metrics hurt, 1 improved marginally: NDCG +0.0176) → HURTS retrieval

**Headline finding: No caption injection strategy improves retrieval quality over the text-only baseline across this testset. All three caption strategies hurt at least two metrics beyond the noise threshold. R5-A (no caption) is the best and simplest configuration.**

### 5.1 R5-B (subC): Caption as Context Enrichment — Largest Degradation

Replacing Sub-query C with the VLLM caption causes the worst overall degradation: −6.6 pp CR, −18.75 pp HR@6, −8.6 pp P@6. Sub-query C was designed to encode case-level contextual information — the patient's clinical notes and special circumstances. Replacing this with a generic image caption discards the case-level text signal entirely in the Sub-query C slot and substitutes it with a wound-type-level visual description that does not vary across cases of the same wound type. The result is that cases whose notes carried distinctive retrieval information (e.g., specific dressing mentions, antibiotic flags, NPWT indications) lose their strongest lexical anchors, and the caption's generic visual vocabulary fails to compensate.

Additionally, R5-B has the highest CtxTok (2128), meaning the caption increases context length without improving content relevance — a cost-only tradeoff.

### 5.2 R5-C (subD): Caption as New 4th Axis — Closest to Neutral

Adding the caption as a new Sub-query D while preserving the original R1-C axes intact is the most conservative strategy. IR metrics (HR@6, MRR, NDCG@6, R@6) are exactly preserved from R5-A, confirming the original three axes already saturate the useful retrieval space at k=6. The small CR loss (−1.2 pp) and P@6 loss (−2.4 pp) suggest the additional caption-driven results introduced some noise — lower-relevance chunks displacing higher-relevance ones when the k=6 cap is applied. Latency is also the highest of all four versions (+44.1 ms over baseline), reflecting the overhead of an additional sub-query execution. R5-C is the least harmful caption strategy but still fails to improve any metric.

### 5.3 R5-D (subBC): Caption Enriching Both B and C — Worst Coverage

Injecting the caption into both Sub-query B and Sub-query C causes a catastrophic R@6 drop (−22.0 pp) alongside a −8.1 pp CR loss. The R@6 collapse is especially notable: R@6 measures the fraction of reference chunks recalled across all k=6 positions. Enriching two sub-queries with the same visual information appears to bias the embedding retrieval toward a narrow cluster of visually-described chunks, displacing the diverse multi-axis coverage that R1-C was designed to achieve. R5-D's unique NDCG@6 improvement (+1.8 pp) is attributable to this narrowing: fewer chunks are recalled, but the ones that are retrieved are ranked more precisely — a classic precision-coverage tradeoff that ultimately makes R5-D unsuitable for the multi-source synthesis task VerdaSense requires. Its lower CtxTok (1766) is the only operational advantage, and it comes at an unacceptable recall cost.

## 6. Per-Case CR Analysis and Hard Cases

The per-case heatmap reveals nuanced patterns beneath the aggregate losses. Caption strategies help on specific case subtypes while hurting on others — the aggregate loss masks selective benefit.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cat_a_type4_wet_infected | 0.8182 | 1.0000 | R5-B | +0.18 | Caption correctly captures infection + moisture markers, aligning with guideline language |

| cat_d_notes_diabetic_nonhealing | 0.7143 | 1.0000 | R5-C | +0.29 | Diabetic foot: visual tissue composition complements sparse clinical notes |

These cases share a common profile: they involve wound types where the visual appearance is the primary diagnostic discriminant, and the original R1-C notes-based sub-query carries limited or neutral information. For such cases, caption injection genuinely adds retrieval signal by grounding the query in tissue-level vocabulary that matches guideline text. Notably, the benefit concentrates in R5-C (additive new axis) and R5-B (direct substitution where notes are sparse), confirming that the caption's value is additive when notes are weak, not when they are strong.

| cat_b_skin_tear_fragile ★ | 0.9474 | 0.1250 | R5-B | −0.82 | Caption describes visual tissue; skin tear retrieval depends on wound mechanism vocabulary absent from image |
| cat_b_skin_tear_type2_flap ★ | 0.6250 | 0.1429 | R5-B | −0.48 | Same mechanism — visual caption displaces the specific "flap", "fragile skin", "tear" vocabulary needed |

The cases where captions hurt are mechanistic wound types — skin tears, burns, post-operative wounds — where the correct guideline chunks are retrieved via wound mechanism and anatomical vocabulary (not tissue composition). The caption, which describes tissue appearance, actively displaces or dilutes this mechanism-level vocabulary. This is a structural incompatibility: visual captions are good at encoding *what the wound looks like* but not *how it was caused*, which is frequently the key retrieval dimension for guideline-specific questions.

### 6.3 Persistent Hard Case

**cat_d_notes_infection_override (WT04):** CR = 0.0000 across all four versions in every run. This case represents a fundamental testset difficulty unrelated to caption strategy — the retrieval index does not contain the specific override-context chunks that constitute this case's ground truth. Caption injection neither solves nor worsens this case; it remains a KB coverage gap.

## 7. RAGAS Metric Deep-Dive

### 7.1 Context Recall (CR) — Primary Metric

| Version | CR Mean | CR SD | Per-Run CR |
|---|---|---|---|
| R5-A | **0.8971** | 0.0112 | [0.8841, 0.9042, 0.9029] |
| R5-B | 0.8311 | 0.0203 | [0.8267, 0.8533, 0.8134] |
| R5-C | 0.8855 | **0.0049** | [0.8907, 0.8810, 0.8848] |
| R5-D | 0.8163 | 0.0179 | [0.8235, 0.7960, 0.8295] |

R5-A achieves the highest mean CR. R5-C is the closest caption strategy (−1.2 pp), with notably lower SD (0.0049) than R5-A (0.0112) — meaning the additive subD strategy produces more stable RAGAS judgements, likely because the original sub-queries remain intact and provide consistent retrieval regardless of how the caption-driven D axis performs in any given run. R5-B and R5-D show larger CR losses and moderately elevated SD, reflecting the instability introduced by caption substitution into core query axes.

### 7.2 Context Precision (CP) — Noise Metric

All four versions achieve CP > 0.97. The caption strategies incur minor CP degradation (−0.1 to −0.4 pp), which is within RAGAS judge stochasticity for most versions. The practical interpretation is that caption injection does not significantly increase the proportion of irrelevant chunks in the retrieved context — the noise cost is low. The precision problem in R5-B and R5-D is better characterised as a *recall* problem: they fail to retrieve the right chunks, rather than retrieving too many wrong ones.

### 7.3 Ranking Metrics (HR@6, MRR, NDCG@6)

R5-B and R5-D drop HR@6 from 0.9375 to 0.7500, a −18.75 pp absolute degradation. This means roughly 1 in 4 cases no longer has any relevant chunk in the top-6 results when the caption replaces or heavily enriches the sub-queries. R5-C preserves HR@6 and MRR exactly (0.9375 and 0.7844 respectively), confirming that the additive approach avoids displacing the chunks that the original R1-C query structure anchors in the top positions.

R5-D's NDCG@6 (0.7447, +1.8 pp over baseline) is the sole positive caption contribution across all IR metrics. As noted in Section 5.3, this reflects a precision-over-coverage tradeoff: the narrowed retrieval space yields better-ranked chunks from a smaller set. For a multi-source synthesis task, this tradeoff is unfavourable.

### 7.4 Coverage Metrics (R@6, P@6)

R5-D's R@6 collapse (0.1519, −22.0 pp) is the most alarming result in R5. The system retrieves fewer unique reference chunks when the caption enriches both B and C axes — a manifestation of the retrieval homogenisation problem. With two sub-queries both anchored to the same visual description, the result pool is dominated by chunks matching the image vocabulary, and the diverse guideline coverage that multi-axis sub-queries achieve is lost.

## 8. Operational Considerations

### 8.1 Latency and Context Budget

| Version | CtxTok | Δ vs R5-A | Lat (ms) | Δ vs R5-A |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R5-A | 1910 | — | 77.0 | — |
| R5-B | 2128 | +217.5 | 94.9 | +17.9 |
| R5-C | 2063 | +152.6 | 121.1 | +44.1 |
| R5-D | 1766 | −144.7 | 111.8 | +34.8 |

Caption injection increases latency by 18–44 ms over the already-fast R5-A baseline (77 ms), purely from the additional sub-query execution overhead. The caption generation itself (8–12 seconds per image) is a one-time offline cost per wound type in the current design; it would become an online cost in a real deployment where captions are generated at inference time from patient photographs. This latency profile — 8–12 seconds per inference call to the VLLM — would be operationally prohibitive in a clinical setting without caching.

R5-D is the most token-efficient version (1766 CtxTok, −144.7 tokens vs baseline), but as established above, its R@6 and CR losses make this efficiency gain meaningless.

### 8.2 VLLM Caption Generation: Infrastructure Considerations

The use of `qwen/qwen-2.5-vl-72b-instruct` via OpenRouter introduces two practical dependencies absent from the R1–R4 pipeline: a commercial API cost per caption and an external network dependency. In the current experimental design, this cost is amortised across all test cases sharing the same wound type, but a production deployment would require either (a) per-case captioning at inference time, introducing 8–12 s latency per query, or (b) an offline caption cache keyed by wound type, losing the per-case visual specificity that makes captions valuable in the first place. Neither option is clearly satisfactory, and this tension between clinical personalisation and operational feasibility is a fundamental design challenge for multimodal RAG in wound care.

## 9. Are the R5 Ablation Experiments Meaningful?

### 9.1 Affirmative: What R5 Contributes

R5 produces a clear and reproducible null result: **caption injection does not improve retrieval quality over the text-only BGE-large baseline for this wound care guideline KB.** Null results are scientifically valid and practically important — they prevent the unnecessary adoption of a more complex, higher-latency, more costly pipeline component that provides no retrieval benefit. R5 is meaningful in three respects:

1. **It answers a genuine research question.** Whether multimodal grounding benefits retrieval in structured clinical RAG has not been systematically tested in the wound care domain. R5 provides direct empirical evidence.

2. **It identifies conditions under which captions help.** The per-case heatmap reveals that captions improve specific wound subtypes (granulating, infected, visually distinctive) while harming others (skin tears, burns, post-operative wounds). This subgroup signal is actionable for future work.

3. **It validates the Stage 1 configuration.** R5-A's successful cross-check against R4-B confirms that the Stage 1 pipeline (BGE-large + Dense + k=6 + R1-C) is reproducible across experimental sessions and that the RAGAS judge is stable.

### 9.2 Limitations

**L1 — Static per-wound-type captions.** The most significant limitation. All test cases sharing the same wound type index receive an identical caption, regardless of case-level clinical context. This prevents the experiment from testing whether *case-specific* visual captions (generated from actual patient photographs submitted with the query) could improve retrieval. The results may underestimate the potential benefit of truly personalised visual grounding.

**L2 — Simulated visual grounding.** The wound type images (WT01–WT08) are representative archetypes, not actual clinical photographs submitted by real users. The captions describe these archetypes, not the diversity of real wounds within each type. Real clinical photographs would show considerable intra-type variation in tissue composition, size, and severity that the archetype captions cannot capture.

**L3 — Caption-to-query alignment mismatch.** Visual captions describe *appearance* (tissue type, colour, exudate). The R1-C sub-query structure was designed to retrieve by *dressing mechanism* (Sub-query B) and *clinical context* (Sub-query C). These are partially orthogonal semantic spaces. The VLLM caption adds appearance vocabulary to mechanism- and context-oriented sub-queries, creating a semantic mismatch that likely contributes to the retrieval degradation. A caption prompt specifically designed to elicit dressing-mechanism-relevant language from the image might perform better.

**L4 — KB vocabulary not optimised for visual descriptors.** The knowledge base chunks (from EWMA, ISTAP, ANZBA, etc.) are written in clinical guideline prose — they do not frequently use appearance vocabulary (e.g., "greenish slough", "perilesional erythema") in the same passages that recommend specific dressings. The embedding space therefore cannot efficiently bridge visual description and clinical recommendation, regardless of how well the caption is written.

**L5 — Small testset (32 cases, 8 wound type prototypes).** With 32 test cases mapping to 8 wound type archetypes, the statistical power to detect small caption benefits is limited. A per-case positive signal observed for 4–5 cases may be swamped by the aggregate loss across the remaining 27–28.

**L6 — Single VLLM model.** Only one captioner (`qwen/qwen-2.5-vl-72b-instruct`) was evaluated. Different VLLMs with different strengths in clinical image description (e.g., MedPaLM-M, LLaVA-Med) might produce captions that align more closely with guideline vocabulary and perform better.

## 10. Recommendations Before G4 (Generation Stage with VLM Caption Involvement)

The R5 findings have direct implications for the design of G4, the generation-stage ablation that is expected to incorporate VLLM captions in the generation prompt rather than (or in addition to) the retrieval query.

### 10.1 Do Not Carry Captions Forward into the Retrieval Layer

R5 establishes clearly that caption injection into retrieval sub-queries hurts more than it helps at the testset level. **The Stage 1 final configuration (R5-A: no caption, BGE-large, Dense, k=6, R1-C) should be used unchanged for all G4 retrieval.** Captions for G4 should operate exclusively at the generation layer — passed directly to the generation LLM/VLM as additional context alongside the retrieved chunks, not used to modify the retrieval query.

### 10.2 Redesign Caption Use as Generation-Layer Context

The correct multimodal architecture for VerdaSense, supported by R5's evidence, is:

```
[Visual input] → VLLM caption → [Generation context only]
[Structured T.I.M.E. input] → R1-C sub-queries → Retrieval → [Retrieved chunks]
                                                                    ↓
                                           [Generator: retrieved chunks + caption + T.I.M.E. payload]
```

This preserves the retrieval integrity established in R1–R4 while allowing the VLLM's visual observations to enrich the generation step directly. At the generation stage, appearance vocabulary is appropriate — the generator must describe wound condition in its output recommendation — whereas at the retrieval stage, it creates a semantic mismatch with guideline prose.

### 10.3 Consider Caption Prompting Redesign for Any Future Retrieval Tests

If a future experiment re-investigates caption-enriched retrieval, the caption prompt should be redesigned to elicit dressing-relevant language rather than appearance-only descriptions. For example: "Describe this wound in terms of the dressing properties it requires (moisture management, antimicrobial, debridement support, exudate absorption), not its visual appearance." This would produce captions whose vocabulary more closely aligns with the guideline KB's embedding space, potentially avoiding the mismatch identified in L3.

### 10.4 Prioritise Case-Specific Captions in G4 Design

For G4, the VLLM should generate a fresh caption per test case using the case's wound type image **and** any available clinical context (notes, T.I.M.E. scores) as joint input. This removes the static-caption limitation (L1) and provides the generation model with personalised visual grounding rather than the archetype-level descriptions used in R5.

### 10.5 Validate the Hard Case Pattern in G4

The persistent zero-CR hard case (`cat_d_notes_infection_override`) and the skin-tear cases should be monitored specifically in G4. For skin tear cases, the generation model may benefit from being explicitly informed that the wound mechanism (tear) overrides any visual tissue composition in the recommendation logic — a reasoning correction that cannot be achieved at the retrieval stage.

### 10.6 Acknowledge the Complexity-Performance Tradeoff in the FYP

R5 demonstrates that adding multimodal complexity (VLLM, caption generation, modified sub-queries, increased CtxTok, higher latency) can degrade rather than improve a well-tuned retrieval pipeline. This is an important FYP contribution: the simpler text-only system outperforms the multimodal-augmented system at the retrieval stage. For the viva, this finding should be framed as a **disciplined negative result** — it is evidence that the Stage 1 pipeline is well-optimised and not a criticism of the multimodal approach in general.

## 11. Decision: R5-A (No Caption) Selected — Stage 1 Configuration Final

**R5-A (no caption, BGE-large-en-v1.5 baseline) is confirmed as the final Stage 1 caption strategy.**

Justification:
- **Highest CR (0.8971)** — the primary retrieval metric
- **Highest HR@6 (0.9375), MRR (0.7844), R@6 (0.3722), P@6 (0.3464)** — best on all IR metrics except NDCG@6
- **Lowest latency (77.0 ms)** — no overhead from sub-query augmentation
- **Lowest CtxTok (1910)** — most efficient context window use
- **No caption generation dependency** — eliminates VLLM API call from the retrieval path
- **Formally selected in R5_summary.json** under `optimal_caption_selection`

The final Stage 1 configuration carried forward into Stage 2 (Generation Ablation) is:

## 13. Discussion — Implications for VerdaSense Architecture

### 13.1 Why Visual Captions Do Not Help Retrieval for Guideline RAG

The R5 null result has a principled mechanistic explanation. The wound care guideline KB contains chunks organised around **clinical recommendations and treatment logic** — they describe when to use a dressing, what properties to select for, and how to manage a wound type. The vocabulary of these chunks is functional (e.g., "high absorbency for moderate-to-heavy exudate", "antimicrobial dressing for infected wound", "non-adherent dressing for fragile periwound skin") rather than observational. VLLM captions, generated from photographs, describe **appearance** (tissue composition percentages, colour, edge morphology). These are different semantic registers, and BGE-large's embedding space is not a bridge between them.

A secondary contributor is the one-to-many mapping problem: one caption serves all cases of a given wound type. Cases within the same wound type but with distinct clinical context (infection override, NPWT adjunct, diabetic comorbidity) are not distinguished by the static caption, so the caption cannot add case-level specificity — only type-level appearance.

### 13.2 When Captions Would Help: A Hypothesis for Future Work

The per-case analysis (Section 6.1) identifies a subset of cases where captions improve retrieval. These cases share two properties: (a) the wound type image accurately represents the case's clinical situation, and (b) the R1-C notes sub-query contains limited case-specific vocabulary. This suggests a **conditional caption injection rule** as a future retrieval strategy: inject the caption into Sub-query C *only* when the notes field is empty or below a vocabulary threshold, and suppress it when notes carry strong case-level signal. This adaptive strategy would avoid the regression on note-rich cases while preserving the benefit on note-sparse cases.

### 13.3 The Appropriate Role for Captions in VerdaSense: Generation, Not Retrieval

R5 reframes where multimodal inputs belong in the VerdaSense architecture. Captions are valuable at the generation stage for two reasons: (i) they provide the generation model with a grounded visual description that can anchor the recommendation in observed wound characteristics, and (ii) they enable the generation model to produce more precise, image-consistent justifications for dressing selection. These are generation-quality benefits, not retrieval benefits, and should be evaluated in G4 with RAGAS answer faithfulness and answer relevance metrics rather than context recall and precision.

**Four things to remember for the viva:**

1. **R5 is a disciplined null result, not a failed experiment.** It empirically rules out a natural hypothesis (visual grounding improves retrieval) and redirects multimodal effort to the generation stage where captions are mechanistically appropriate.

2. **The per-case heatmap shows captions help on visually distinctive wound subtypes but hurt on mechanism-specific ones.** Skin tears and burns retrieve by wound cause, not tissue appearance; captions that describe tissue appearance displace the mechanism vocabulary these cases depend on.

R5 tests a simplified version of the multimodal hypothesis; a stronger test requires per-case visual inputs.
